# Notes — how this course works

**Twenty minutes, so budget forty.** Seven `##` steps, all reading. No polad, no code,
no practice repo. The only prerequisite is the portal being reachable and Employee
Login working.

The opener. It belongs to no stretch and has no theme.

## The one thing that doesn't get cut

**Step 5 — write down what you think has to be built.** Cut steps 3 and 4 before this
one. It is the only step that produces an artifact the rest of the week can argue
with, and its whole value is that it happens *before* anyone is told anything.

Step 6 is the second thing that does not get cut, but it is short.

## Before: what you set up

Nothing technical. But say two sentences before they start, and no more:

**Hand off on:** *"This is the engagement. Read it the way you would read a handover
from a contractor who has left. I will answer questions after."*

**The belief you plant, and it is a true one:** that this is orientation. It is. What
they will not expect is that the orientation is also the lab, and that step 6 changes
what they think the week is.

**Do not preface it with the framing.** Explaining that Theoria is a device before
they read the letter defuses the exact thing worth feeling in steps 1 through 5.

## After: what you land

Step 6 is written to be read, not delivered — but you will want to say some of it
aloud anyway, and that is fine as long as it comes **after** they have read it rather
than instead.

The sentence worth adding in your own words is the one about the surplus: they will be
shown more than the work needs, on purpose, and that is a promise rather than an
accident. Everything later in the week depends on that promise having been made out
loud once.

## Going wrong on purpose

**Nothing staged.** The material is already wrong in the ways it is supposed to be —
the inventory is stale, the thread has degraded, the API catalog documents a field the
service does not always send.

**Do not point at any of it.** The catalog's `reason` field in particular: it is
discovered in `10-`, and naming it here converts a finding into a fact.

## Where it goes wrong

- **They skim.** Most likely failure by a distance. The steps gate, which helps, but
  somebody will click through four screens in ninety seconds. The parking lot is the
  answer, not chasing them.
- **They ask what the app is before step 5.** Deflect: *"write down what you think it
  is, that is the step."*
- **Somebody has taken the new Web API 100** and recognises the catalog. Good. Ask
  them not to say which field is wrong.
- **The letter reads as too long.** It is four paragraphs. If a room is restless, read
  the last one aloud yourself and let them do the rest.

## The demo this lab wants

**None, and resist it.** Everything here is reading, the room can do it faster than
you can present it, and a walkthrough of the portal converts the one genuinely
self-directed twenty minutes of the week into a lecture.

The only screen worth showing is Employee Login working, once, if anyone is stuck.

## Connective tissue — what this makes legible

- **Forward into everything.** Step 6 is the promise the whole week is measured
  against, and the surplus only reads as design because it was named here first.
- **Step 5's paper, on Friday.** Ask them to get it out. That comparison is the
  cheapest retrospective available and it needs no preparation.

## The seam into `11-`

None directly, and that is deliberate — the opener does not hand off into a technique.
What it hands off into is the first stretch's theme, which nobody says out loud.

## What this lab doesn't clarify

- **What they will actually build.** On purpose. Answering it in step 5 would remove
  the only prediction in the lab.
- **Anything technical whatsoever.** A room that wants to start typing will find this
  frustrating, and it is worth saying that tomorrow is entirely typing.
